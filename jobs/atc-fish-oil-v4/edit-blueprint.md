## Evidence notes

- ATC Healthcare, *ATC Fish Oil*: https://atchealthcare.com.ph/product/atc-fish-oil/ (lists 995 mg fish oil per softgel and vitamin E; page does not quantify EPA or DHA separately).
- Philippine FDA product verification: https://verification.fda.gov.ph/All_FoodProductsview.php?ACCOUNTCODE=FR-4000010802010&export=pdf (food supplement; no approved therapeutic claims).
- NIH ODS, *Omega-3 Fatty Acids: Health Professional Fact Sheet*: https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Omega3FattyAcids-HealthProfessional/ (general EPA/DHA biology; DHA is concentrated in brain and retina; supplement formulations and EPA/DHA amounts vary).
- 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of omega-3 and exercise-induced muscle damage: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13165459/ (some pooled DOMS outcomes favored omega-3, while the review describes overall evidence as equivocal and notes substantial methodological and dosing limitations; not ATC-specific evidence).
- Borzutzky et al., 2024, prospective omega-3 acne intervention: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38982829/ (small dietary intervention without placebo control; not proof that this product treats acne).

Do not infer a per-softgel EPA/DHA dose from the total fish-oil weight. Keep acne and recovery as first-person experience, label them as anecdotal, and state that results vary. Do not imply the supplement treats inflammation, acne, DOMS, or any disease. The ATC product page and FDA record do not authorize therapeutic claims.

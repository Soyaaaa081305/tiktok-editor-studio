## 5. Analyze this recording and its evidence

1. Inventory original media, photos, readable labels and existing project files. Record each asset's path, ID, duration, dimensions, source frame rate and audio properties. Preserve originals and prior exports. Identify focus, lighting, clipping, framing and noisy sections that affect attainable quality.
2. Inspect the entire source timeline, including trailing product/demo footage. Use continuous playback/listening when the tools support it; otherwise use systematic visual coverage, transcription and signal checks. Record exactly which methods were used. A contact sheet is visual sampling; ASR is a provisional transcript. Neither is proof of continuous listening.
3. Transcribe the actual words and preserve Taglish code-switching. Resolve uncertainties in terms such as EPA, DHA, compound names, numbers and brand spelling. Use the recording and readable label rather than guessing from an earlier topic. Remove backstage chatter and false starts without changing meaning.
4. Read every supplied study relevant to a proposed claim. Prefer publisher full text, PubMed Central or an accessible author manuscript; use verified abstracts when that is the available evidence. Keep access level visible in the notes. Follow linked studies, check contradictory/null findings and current correction/retraction notices before adding science.
5. Reuse a supplied research package's sources and IDs. Recheck material claims and link status; do not redo broad research for an already verified unchanged claim without a reason. New claims need their own evidence. Cache reusable reference style, not permanently frozen scientific truth.
6. If no research package exists, conduct a focused search for the central claim using PubMed and original study/publisher pages. Use 1–3 defensible sources where available. Count a review/meta-analysis as a synthesis and an experiment as primary research; label them correctly. Do not count a creator's explanation as an additional clinical study.
7. Keep general ingredient evidence, evidence about the exact marketed product, readable package statements and my personal experience distinct. Verify EPA/DHA amounts, elemental versus compound mass, blend total versus single-ingredient dose, per-serving units and capsule counts before showing them prominently. Price comparisons require current comparable quantities and an actual source. Never infer clinical efficacy from a product label or a mechanism animation.
8. A misleading recorded claim needs a coherent documented cut, a clear correction where feasible, or a specific source limitation. Tiny qualifiers cannot repair an inaccurate audible promise. Never invent replacement speech or an anecdote. Preserve important uncertainty while shortening.

Keep long citations, source paths, licensing and production rationale in accompanying notes. A brief readable author/year credit belongs on an actual evidence beat. Essential qualifiers remain in the spoken or visible explanation when omission would mislead.

## 6. Story, pacing and picture selection

Build the paper edit from the words. Identify the first useful hook, the early payoff, the one main explanation, the evidence boundary and the practical ending. Remove padding and duplicated points. Preserve breaths and complete consonants; cover necessary dialogue jumps with a motivated change of framing or a relevant insert.

Choose among these modes according to the spoken idea, with no percentage or effects quota:

- **Presenter:** Full-screen camera for hook, opinion, explanation reset, personal experience and conclusion. Use a modest 1.03–1.10 punch-in on a meaningful turn when the source supports it. No constant drift or automatic zoom every few seconds.
- **Stacked:** Upper approximately 35–50% contains a large relevant image, B-roll, study extract or original graphic. Lower contains the synchronized continuing presenter. Specify both crops, not just two rectangles. Keep eyes and mouth visible. Change the upper insert when the noun, action, evidence or comparison changes.
- **Full-screen insert:** Let real demonstration, product detail, evidence or graph occupy the canvas when that is the current point. Do not put an important insert in a small floating card. Return to the presenter when it improves understanding or connection.
- **Full-screen explanation:** Show a relationship or mechanism that footage alone cannot explain, with a simple readable build and a clear payoff. This can be sophisticated in timing and construction while remaining visually restrained.
- **Keyword beat:** Promote a meaningful term such as FISH OIL, EPA or DHA into a large foreground typographic beat. Give the word a reason, a safe position and time to be read. Avoid adding another headline that repeats the same caption.

Direct cuts are the normal transition. Prefer action matches, subject matches, short masks or a brief dissolve for a genuine shift. Use a whip only when direction and meaning justify it. Preserve continuous lower-presenter timing when only the top insert changes. A transition has a planned entrance, landing and exit; it must not consume the explanation's reading time.

Choose B-roll by relevance and image quality, not by novelty. If assets are limited, vary a genuine crop, readable label detail, source excerpt or original accurate diagram. Do not reuse one bottle shot across unrelated claims simply to fill every second. List optional additional pickup shots after delivering the best supported result.

**Hook-to-finish effort curve:** Give the first ten seconds the strongest story decisions: first-frame cover, immediate moving hook, one early answer or open loop, purposeful crop changes and the most useful motion-graphic reveal. Keep the middle clear and balanced; tie each insert change to the exact current noun, action or contrast. Let the ending tighten again for the takeaway, requested CTA and Noda.lifts spoken signature. This is an effort curve, not a cut-rate quota. Never accelerate unrelated cuts simply to make the opening busy.

## 7. Motion-graphics design contract

For each explanatory graphic, decide:

1. **Question answered:** What should the viewer understand by its final state?
2. **Grounding:** Which recorded phrase, source ID, datum or label supports it?
3. **Construction:** Exact text, objects, connectors, data, layers and geometry.
4. **Build:** What appears first, how it moves and the frame of the spoken trigger.
5. **Hold:** The readable settled state; enough time to understand it.
6. **Exit:** Direct cut, fade or motivated transition; exact duration and overlap.
7. **Sound:** A specific cue or an intentional quiet event, justified by the landing.

**Narration coverage rule — critical:** A full-screen takeover changes the picture layer, never the dialogue layer. Keep the selected original speech playing beneath presenter, stacked, product, evidence and motion-graphic scenes. Before editing, align the actual recording to the transcript and build the audio EDL first. For each visual beat, record the exact source speech range that motivates it. Concatenate retained speech at its intended natural pace, preserving complete words and meaning. Do not insert silence, hold a graphic after its line ends, mute the presenter because the B-roll fills the canvas, or treat SFX as dialogue coverage. A visual can settle while the sentence continues.

If a beat has no suitable recorded line, use a different truthful line already in the source, shorten or remove the beat, or mark the exact pickup line needed for a future recording. Never invent, synthesize or clone narration. Keep authentic breaths and pauses that are part of the useful performance, but label each intentional silent span in the EDL with source range, duration and reason. The default authored silence list is empty except for the 0.1-second cover. If a graphic needs extra reading time, shorten its copy or keep relevant B-roll visible under continuing speech; do not add a silent explanatory hold.

Use one primary idea per visual. Depth, masks, line draws, diagram transformations, evidence highlights and coordinated reveals are available when they teach the point. Keep easing controlled and let the result settle. Typical small reveals are 0.10–0.25 seconds; readable holds are chosen from text length and complexity. These are starting points, not a timing template imposed on every topic.

For a study graphic, retrieve real data, preserve axes/units/comparator and important uncertainty, and cite it. Recreate an original chart from verified values when suitable. Do not fabricate percentages, error bars or a trial screenshot. A conceptual diagram is not measured evidence. Make that distinction clear through its wording or a concise label only when needed to avoid confusion.

For Chris Beardsley material, read a genuinely accessible post or user-supplied excerpt and follow its cited research. Treat the post as interpretation. Do not claim to have read locked content or reproduce a Patreon graph merely because its thumbnail is visible. Use an authorized supplied figure with attribution or draw an original diagram/chart grounded in accessible data; preserve uncertainty and figure meaning. Keep the visual plan useful if the post is unavailable.

Remove decorative orbits, stock cartoon organs, fake physiological waves, arbitrary molecule icons, neon glows, floating bottle stickers, generic dashboards, repetitive rounded cards and unmotivated particles. Do not add canvas labels such as Actual product, Genuine B-roll, Ingredient 01 or Inside the bottle. Every remaining line or effect must help the viewer identify, understand, qualify or act.

## 8. Typography, lighter captions and visual finish

### Stable visual vocabulary; flexible composition

Default to Inter, with two deliberate editorial weights: 600 for dialogue/body and 700 for headline emphasis. Use the actual available font files and verify loading. White/dark text, neutral backgrounds and one restrained warm-gold accent (#F1C75B) form the base. A source or product can justify a supporting color. Avoid adding several accent colors merely to distinguish cards.

At 1080 × 1920, starting sizes are 48–58 px dialogue, 42–56 px essential supporting information and 96–144 px hero keywords. Important qualifiers should generally be at least 36–44 px; an author/year credit can be smaller if it is legible at phone size and holds long enough. Measure the actual text and rephrase concise copy before shrinking. Scale typography proportionally for another resolution.

### Caption behavior

- Caption the final audible dialogue accurately, in short phrases, generally 3–7 words and at most two lines. Use sentence case for ordinary dialogue. Preserve intelligibility; break long technical phrases naturally. Avoid word-by-word flashing or a boxed subtitle paragraph.
- Use a subtle dark outline/shadow only as needed for the background. Start with about 2 px outline at 1080 width; inspect contrast. A full opaque caption box and extra-heavy type are optional fixes, not defaults.
- Place captions in negative space using a small consistent set of anchors. Default to the lower-middle region around y=1280, centered within x=80–900. Move the anchor at a scene/semantic boundary when it would otherwise cover eyes, mouth, evidence or a product label. Keep it stable during the phrase; do not let it wander frame by frame.
- Highlight an important word selectively, using color or a restrained 10–20% enlargement when useful. Most words have no special animation. Large keyword scenes can replace that term's ordinary caption rendering at the same time; retain the spoken wording once in a readable form and avoid three simultaneous copies.
- Caption timing comes from final speech, not the draft script. Keep word data in Remotion Caption-compatible JSON with source and output timing. Regroup pages after dialogue cuts. Check first/last words, acronyms, punctuation and silence boundaries.

### Mobile layout

Use x≈80–900 and y≈160–1640 as starting key-content margins at 1080 × 1920; reserve the right action rail and lower UI. These are house working margins, not a device guarantee. Inspect a phone-sized preview and a simulated UI mask. Adapt to an actual available platform preview and label unperformed native TikTok checks honestly.

### Color and authenticity

Match exposure and white balance between shots. Preserve natural skin, package and room colors. Use a neutral Rec.709 SDR finish, controlled contrast and restrained saturation. Do not apply the same dramatic LUT to every source. Avoid artificial smoothing, sharpened halos, crushed shadows, clipped highlights or unsupported HDR tags. The clean look comes from choices in the edit rather than synthetic replacement people or products.

## 9. Opening cover and ending

Make a cover from a genuine strong frame or supplied photo, with a concise truthful headline and restrained typography. All its elements must be visible on frame 0. No synthetic face, synthetic product or delayed title build.

Hold it clearly for approximately 0.1 second: calculate `coverFrames = max(1, round(0.1 * outputFps))`, ordinarily 3 frames at 30 fps or 6 at 60 fps. Start the moving hook and its dialogue immediately afterward. If appropriate, overlay a short dissolve or matching scale transition of about 0.1 second over that already-moving hook. Do not add another static/silent pause. Count the overlap once and keep dialogue/caption/SFX timing coherent. Save frame 0 as a separate PNG.

The embedded opening supplies an available cover frame. It cannot force TikTok to choose that frame automatically. Keep the posting note brief: choose the opening frame in Select cover when needed. The 0.1-second duration is our editing preference, not an official platform minimum.

Finish with a useful takeaway and one context-appropriate CTA. Educational posts can ask a concrete question or invite a save. Product posts can use a truthful requested yellow-basket CTA. Do not add a YouTube subscribe animation to a TikTok export or invent discounts, urgency or endorsements. No platform watermark/outro copied from a reference.

1. **Intake and manifest:** source files, hashes, video/audio metadata, project/config, available fonts/elements, language, platform and current user brief.

## 12. Dialogue and SFX; no music by default

Keep my original voice at natural pace. Repair noise and uneven levels conservatively; avoid metallic denoising, pumping, missing breaths or clipped syllables. Smooth cut ambience and use corrective processing only when the source needs it. Lock speech continuity before adding picture: an overlay is not a reason to remove narration. Do not pad a short source with silent B-roll; finish concisely or identify a future pickup line.

Choose a restrained consistent SFX palette: short air/cloth-style whoosh for a meaningful movement, soft tick/click for an evidence or diagram landing, modest pop for a rare keyword accent. Avoid repeated meme sounds, trailer booms and a sound on every word. Choose and record a real licensed/owned asset for each cue. A useful major transition or explanatory reveal should receive its planned cue; do not accidentally deliver a silent effects track.

Align the sound's audible attack/peak with the visual landing rather than blindly using its file start. Trim dead air and long tails, fade edges, and balance against dialogue. Peaks around -18 to -14 dBFS for an isolated accent can be a starting point, not a substitute for listening or a universal rule. Duck a cue if it masks speech. Music stays disabled: no generated or downloaded bed, ambient melody, intro sting or outro tune by default. Preserve useful non-musical room ambience; note unavoidable music embedded in the original speech if it cannot be removed cleanly.

Use one finished soundtrack shared by preview and export, or equivalent deterministic common mix logic. If the picture render is muted, explicitly mux the verified soundtrack. Confirm the actual delivered file contains its audio and planned cue events. Cue-window energy proves signal presence, not perceptual audibility; listen when supported and record the difference. Compare the export to the intended mix, accounting for encoding, rather than checking only the Studio icons.

Aim at -16 to -14 LUFS integrated and true peak at or below -1.5 dBTP in the final encoded file. These are house mix targets, not a TikTok certification. Measure the actual file and correct clipping/level issues. Do not infer final loudness from a volume setting.

## 13. Quality review and definition of done

Run a content-removal pass before export: remove redundant titles, repeated caption/headline/CTA text, unnecessary labels, decorative diagrams and gratuitous sounds. Review the remaining explanation at phone size. Then inspect the actual final export, not just a still render or the plan.

Use these statuses with evidence: **PASS**, **REVISE**, **BLOCKED**, **UNVERIFIED**, **NOT APPLICABLE**. An unperformed check never earns a pass. Fix every remediable critical defect, render the affected changes and inspect them again. Do not create arbitrary perfection loops or stop solely because a self-awarded score is high.

### Critical checks

- **Meaning and evidence:** coherent retained speech; correctly qualified claims; real sources; no invented numbers, fabricated evidence, altered testimony or missing material caveats.
- **Brief:** current Taglish/topic/CTA/duration; minimal natural style; lighter adaptive captions; no added music; meaningful SFX; authentic 0.1s cover and immediate hook.
- **Picture:** source-matched B-roll; valid top/bottom synchronization; full-frame shots fill their canvas; readable charts/labels; natural color; no unintended empty border, stretched face, clipped type, occluded mouth or important evidence.
- **Timing:** accurate dialogue captions and semantic visual triggers; complete words; transition overlaps correct; no stale caption during unrelated silence, frozen intro or accidental end gap.
- **Narration coverage:** every visual beat maps to an audible source line or explicit pause; full-screen graphics and B-roll retain continuous dialogue; the only added silent time is the listed 0.1-second cover; pause audit flags are reviewed and unexplained dead air is repaired.
- **Audio:** valid source/mix; planned SFX actually included; intelligible voice; no clips/clicks; measured final LUFS/true peak; no accidental muting or default music track.
